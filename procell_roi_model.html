Reading ROI as years. ROI (years to recover) = fixed capital ÷ annual EBITDA — how many years of operating cash flow it takes to earn back the capital. Fewer years is better; a loss-making price never recovers. At the model's break-even fiber price the recovery is ~8.5 years (1 ÷ capital-recovery factor), consistent with the 20-yr / 10% basis.
Feedstock chain. Yeast (dry t/yr) = trucks/day × days/week × weeks/year × gallons × L/gal × density × solids%. At the defaults, one truck = 5,000 gal × 3.785 L/gal × 1.0 kg/L × 10% = 1.89 t dry; over 350 delivery days ≈ 662 t/yr per truck/day, so 8.6 trucks/day ≈ the base 5,700 t/yr.
Economics. Fiber is the only saleable product (DSP protein powder is an internal intermediate). Plant scale S = yeast ÷ base; fiber output = 6,750 × S; revenue = price × output. Variable OpEx scales ×S; fixed OpEx and fixed capital scale ×S0.6 (six-tenths rule). EBITDA = revenue − OpEx. Bigger scale lowers per-tonne cost, so recovery gets faster as trucks/day rises.
Notes: the source workbook is a single-point estimate at 6,750 t/yr; the 0.6 scaling exponents and truck-conversion factors are added here (all editable). Spent yeast is treated as a $0 waste stream by default. Excludes income tax, depreciation shields, incentives, product shipping, sales/G&A/R&D. Study-level accuracy ≈ ±30%. Figures are illustrative.
